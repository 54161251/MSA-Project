# Ironleaf R4848 CNC Router: Operator Manual

Fictional manual written for the MSA demo. Ironleaf is not a real company.

[p.1]
## Specifications
- Cutting area: 1220 mm x 1220 mm
- Footprint: 1.8 m x 1.7 m
- Spindle: 2.2 kW, 6,000 to 24,000 rpm
- Power: 240 V, 20 A
- Dust collection port: 100 mm

[p.2]
## Safety warnings
- The spindle can throw chips and broken bits. Stay out of the cutting area while it runs.
- Never reach into the machine while the spindle is turning.
- Never leave the router running unattended.
- Hold work down firmly with clamps or screws; loose work can be thrown.

## Personal protective equipment
- Wear safety glasses at all times near the router.
- Wear hearing protection while the spindle runs.
- No gloves, loose sleeves or jewelry near the spindle.

[p.3]
## Before you start
- Turn on the dust collector.
- Check the bit is sharp, the right size and tight in the collet.
- Clamp your stock and check clamps are clear of the tool path.

## Operation
- Home the machine and set your work zero.
- Run the job in air first, 25 mm above the stock, to check the path.
- Start the job with one hand near the emergency stop.
- Wait for the spindle to stop fully before opening the enclosure.

[p.4]
## Materials
- Allowed: plywood and MDF.
- Allowed: hardwood and softwood.
- Allowed: acrylic with a single-flute bit.
- Ask staff: aluminum, only with staff present.
- Never: fiberglass or carbon fiber. The dust is hazardous.
- Never: steel.

[p.5]
## Maintenance
- Every 40 hours of use: clean and lubricate the linear rails.
- Every 30 days: empty the dust collector bag.
- Every 90 days: inspect the spindle collet and nut.

## Troubleshooting
- Burn marks: raise the feed rate or use a sharper bit.

[p.6]
## Emergency
- Press the red emergency stop for any problem.
- For a cut or flying debris injury, stop the machine and get first aid; call 911 for serious injuries.
